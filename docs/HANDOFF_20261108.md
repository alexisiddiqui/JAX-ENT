## 0. One-paragraph state

The corrected residue protection-factor targets (k_obs read as ms⁻¹) put Tris aSyn residues 2–94 at
ln PF ≈ 0.0–0.5 (random coil), 90–113 at ≈ 1, and the acidic tail 114–140 at 2–3.4. Tris-MD with default
BV (0.35/2.0) predicts ln PF ≈ 4.5, with the OPPOSITE shape (structured N-terminus, contact-free tail). A
single median shift (the MoPrP recipe) fixes the level but not the shape, and frame reweighting cannot fix
the shape because no frames in Tris-MD have an exposed 10–33 or a protected tail (closed-form NNLS bound and

conditions (done), fit the tail (done), compare to a no-fit baseline (done), use Tris-MD not Control-MD or
Shaw as the model of Tris. Open: what to do about the N-terminal over-protection and tail under-protection
(k_int electrostatics vs. BV contact definition vs. the ensemble), and the Lysosomal time units.

## 2. The experimental data (what they are, how they were computed)

Source: Seetaloo, Zacharopoulou, Stephens, Kaminski Schierle, Phillips, Anal. Chem. 2022,
10.1021/acs.analchem.2c03183 (PMC9730295; full text fetched via NCBI efetch into
`/home/alexi/.claude/jobs/c9a9de6b/tmp/paper.txt` — job tmp, may be gone). SI spreadsheet
`data/_aSyn/ac2c03183_si_002.xlsx` (all sheets dumped to CSV by
`analysis/target_diagnostics/xlsx_to_csv.py`). The SI PDF `ac2c03183_si_001.pdf` could NOT be downloaded
(ACS 403, PMC bot check; the download was blocked) — ask the user to drop it in `data/_aSyn/`.
Methods paper for the software: HDfleX, Seetaloo, Kish, Phillips, Anal. Chem. 2022, PMC9204700.

- ms-HDX-MS (ms2min quench-flow), 20 °C, 20-fold dilution, 30 peptides + ETD c/z fragments. Time grid for
  Tris/Extracellular/Intracellular: 0, 50, 100, 250, 500 ms, 1, 10, 30 s (+ 60 or 300 s last point; see
  `data/_aSyn/uptake/S11_time_grids.json`). Lysosomal: 10 points, 10 s to ~60,000 s (from the intact-protein
  panel S5-right); its absolute grid for the peptide fits is still unconfirmed.
- Buffers (Table 1): Tris 20 mM pH 7.4 no salts; Extracellular 143 Na, 4 K, 2.5 Ca, 0.7 Mg, pH 7.4;
  Intracellular 15 Na, 140 K, 100 nM Ca, 10 Mg, pH 7.2; Lysosomal 20 Na, 60 K, 20 mM citrate pH 4.9.
- Each uptake curve (peptide and fragment) is fitted in HDfleX to a stretched exponential
  N(1 − exp(−(k_obs t)^β)), N = q = exchangeable amides, β ∈ (0,1]. We reproduce every reported k_exp to
  3 decimals on the grid above (`analysis/target_diagnostics/peptide_kinetics.py`,
  `fitting/run_asyn_uptake_sidecars.py` stretched_k). Median β 0.82–0.86.
- UNITS: the "Observed rate constant (s-1)" column of sheet S10 (= `data/_aSyn/aSyn_k_obs.csv`) is ms⁻¹
  for Tris/Extra/Intra (confirmed by refitting the raw curves, ratio 1.000). `data/extract_PF.py` multiplies
  by 1000 (KOBS_PER_MS_TO_PER_S). Extracellular and Intracellular reported rates are additionally multiplied
  by the bradykinin factors 1.179 and 1.722 (= k_brady(Tris)/k_brady(cond)); the raw uptake curves are not.
- LYSOSOMAL UNITS ARE DIFFERENT (user decision pending): peptides are 5 % exchanged at the first point
  (10 s), plateau near 12,000 s; a single-exponential refit gives ~9e-4 s⁻¹ vs reported 6e-3. A rate of 6e-3
  per ms would be complete before the first time point, so the ×1000 is wrong for that column. Current
  Lysosomal residue targets (median ln PF −5.7) carry a spurious ln 1000; read as s⁻¹ they sit near +1.2.
- The residue table is the fragment-level rate "flattened" onto the residues it covers (52 constant-value
  segments, 23 single residues): it is not independent per-residue data. Per-residue SD/k_obs median 0.09.
- HDfleX protection factor = k_int,fit / k_obs,fit where k_int,fit is the same stretched exponential fitted
  to the simulated Bai intrinsic curve of the peptide. The HDfleX authors state aSyn is "incompatible with
  the Bai and Englander model ... all the peptides exchanged faster than the predicted rates"; they therefore
  report bradykinin-normalised k_obs, not PFs. Recomputed HDfleX-style for Tris: peptides 2–94 ln PF
  0.0–0.35, 90–113 ≈ 1.0, tail 2.0–3.4 (`peptide_pf_hdflex.py`).
- Paper's structural claims (what any model should reproduce): after bradykinin normalisation protection is
  Tris > Extracellular > Intracellular everywhere; C-terminal 115–135 exposure correlates with nucleation
  (R < −0.9 with lag time); exposure of N-terminal 10–33, 46–60, the NAC and 101–113 correlates with
  elongation; proposed mechanism = loss of long-range N–C electrostatic contacts when the C-terminal charge
  is screened (Ca²⁺, salts) or neutralised (pH 4.9). Tail protection in our targets falls Tris 2.7 →
  Extracellular 1.2 → Intracellular 0.6 (region means of ln PF).

## 6. Open decisions for the user

1. The shape misfit (N-terminus 2–38 over-protected by ≈ 2 ln, tail under-protected by ≈ 2 ln, in every
   condition, not removable by reweighting). Candidate explanations, each implying a different run:
   (a) Tris-MD's N-terminal structure (humps 7–10, 18–33) is a starting-conformer artefact — test with
   per-replicate / per-start-family fits (rod vs coil vs hairpin; frame index → replicate is
   `frame // 12005`, order rod1-3, coil1-3, hairpin1-3), or an ensemble from a different start;
   (b) tail protection in Tris is electrostatic slowing of k_int (acidic D/E-rich segment, no counter-ions;
   the HDfleX authors already note Bai fails for aSyn) — would need an electrostatic k_int correction, which
   is a modelling decision;
   (c) the BV heavy-atom contact count is the wrong protection proxy for an IDP (ln PF 3–4 for a coil with
   ten neighbours) — scale, different cutoff, or a different forward model.
2. Lysosomal units (s⁻¹ vs ms⁻¹ in the S10 column; needs the SI PDF or a decision). Not fitted this session.
3. Whether the target observable is the raw peptide uptake (current), the paper's condition-difference
   heatmaps (scale-free; Figures 1B/1C, S6B–D in the xlsx = per-residue Δuptake per time point between
   conditions), or the residue PFs (inherit the Bai problem).
4. Multiplicative BV scale (s ≈ 0.10) instead of the additive median shift, if the user wants "scale".
