# HDX noise-model design
## PF-informed joint peptide–time target uncertainty for MoPrP / jaxENT

**Status:** mathematical design freeze for implementation and validation  
**Supersedes:** the earlier per-timepoint `Σ_t` design and the subsequent full peptide–time covariance review  
**Primary scientific assumption:** centroid uptake is predominantly in the EX2 regime  
**Primary statistical object:** a structured covariance over the complete peptide–time vector, not a covariance estimated by treating timepoints as replicate samples

---

## Executive decision

The primary HDX target model will combine:

1. a **strict residue-level EX2 mean**, mapped residue-first into peptide uptake;
2. a **full joint covariance over all peptide–time observations**;
3. persistent PF/model-discrepancy, peptide, timepoint-common, and independent acquisition components;
4. ANM only as a **regularising correlation hypothesis**, with a separately estimated amplitude and explicit null controls;
5. an unnormalised Gaussian or Gaussian-mixture likelihood, including the log-determinant;
6. cross-fitting and held-out predictive scoring, because the available MoPrP data do not contain experimental replicates from which an unrestricted observation covariance could be estimated.

For the current dimensions,

$$
P=14,\qquad T=15,\qquad N=PT=210,
$$

so the primary covariance is

$$
\boxed{\Sigma\in\mathbb R^{210\times210}}.
$$

Per-timepoint $(T,P,P)$ and collapsed $(P,P)$ matrices remain useful **compatibility and ablation outputs**, but they are not the primary statistical model because they discard cross-time covariance generated by a persistent underlying PF discrepancy.

The most physically defensible primary mean is the ordinary EX2 first moment. Free additive increment gains are excluded from the primary model. A smooth interval-hazard relaxation may be tested as a strongly regularised sensitivity analysis, with its PF coefficient fixed to one. If isotopic envelopes show substantial EX1 or mixed EX1/EX2 exchange, the centroid model is rejected in favour of an envelope-level or explicit kinetic-state model.

---

## 1. Context and problem statement

The shipped `Sigma.npz` was constructed as a peptide covariance of uptake curves, effectively treating the 15 timepoints as samples. That construction has three linked problems:

- timepoints are coordinates of a kinetic observation vector, not independent replicate observations;
- a sample covariance from 15 timepoints is rank-limited and cannot identify a general peptide covariance reliably;
- inversion can place excessive precision on directions dominated by sampling jitter rather than by reproducible experimental information.

The replacement therefore must first define its estimand. With no replicate HDX measurements, it must **not** be described as an empirically estimated replicate-noise covariance. It is instead a low-dimensional, model-based combination of:

- uncertainty or discrepancy in the latent PF description;
- uncertainty introduced by mapping residue exchange into overlapping peptide observations;
- persistent peptide-specific procedural effects;
- effects shared by all peptides measured at one labelling time;
- pooled independent peptide–time acquisition noise.

This document calls the result a **PF-informed target/model-discrepancy covariance plus experimental variance model**.

The design is tied to the project pause point in `plans/hdx_moprp_pivot_calibration.md`: no covariance-weighted reweighting arm should be interpreted until this target model has passed its acceptance criteria.

---

## 2. Resolution of the two draft approaches

The first draft used exact EX2 increments but constructed an independent $P\times P$ covariance at each timepoint. The second draft corrected the geometry to a full $PT\times PT$ covariance and introduced persistent peptide and timepoint effects. The final design is a hybrid: it retains the second draft's covariance architecture while returning the primary mean to strict EX2.

| Design issue | Earlier per-timepoint design | Full peptide–time design | Final decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mean at physical anchor | Exact EX2 when all increment gains equal one | Exact EX2 when interval offsets are zero and PF slopes equal one | Strict EX2 is primary |
| Flexible mean | Gains multiply precomputed uptake increments | Complementary-log-log interval hazards | Neither is primary; smooth hazard is sensitivity only |
| Uptake bounds | Free gains can produce cumulative uptake above one | Survival recursion preserves $[0,1]$ | Require bounded latent uptake |
| Unequal time intervals | Handled at the EX2 anchor | Explicit in interval hazards | No distinction at strict EX2 |
| Cross-time PF covariance | Omitted | Retained through stacked propagation | Full cross-time covariance required |
| Peptide-specific effects | Mainly diagonal within each timepoint | Persistent across the peptide's complete curve | Persistent peptide term required |
| Timepoint-common effects | Mean gains or independent noise | Shared loading across all peptides at one time | Structured timepoint term required |
| PF non-identifiability | Marginal multistart variance, usually diagonalised | Modes and within-mode covariance retained | Preserve modes, but calibrate their weights |
| ANM | Direct covariance-like input | Correlation regulariser with separate scale | Correlation hypothesis only |
| Likelihood scale | Often trace-normalised for delivery | Absolute covariance retained | No trace normalisation in primary likelihood |
| Compatibility | Direct use by current loss | Requires a new joint loss | Add new loss; retain legacy exports |

The decisive correction is not the flexible hazard mean. It is recognition that the same latent PF discrepancy affects all points on an uptake curve, creating covariance blocks

$$
\Sigma^{\rm PF}_{jk}=J_jC_zJ_k^\top
$$

for every pair of times $t_j,t_k$, not only for $j=k$.

## 3. Scientific estimand and terminology

### 3.1 Replicate-level definition

For experiment or replicate $e$, peptide $p$, and timepoint $j$, the ideal covariance estimand would be

$$
\Sigma_{(p,j),(q,k)}
=
\operatorname{Cov}_e\!\left[
Y_{epj}-\mu_{pj},
Y_{eqk}-\mu_{qk}
\right].
$$

The present dataset does not contain enough independent experiments to estimate this object non-parametrically. The implemented $\Sigma$ is therefore **model-implied**, with a small number of variance components.

### 3.2 Epistemic and aleatoric components

The final target distribution combines quantities with different meanings:

- **PF inverse uncertainty / target uncertainty:** uncertainty in which residue-level PF field is compatible with the peptide data;
- **structural model discrepancy:** a hypothesis that errors at structurally coupled residues are correlated;
- **experimental variance:** peptide recovery, timing/quench, and acquisition effects;
- **PF multimodality:** separate inverse solutions, represented by separate mixture components rather than by one inflated Gaussian.

These must be named separately in outputs. In particular, the ANM/PF terms must never be labelled simply as “observation noise”.

### 3.3 Target distribution used by reweighting

Let $\mathbf x_\star$ denote the latent target peptide–time uptake surface. The intended target model is

$$
p(\mathbf x_\star\mid y)
=
\sum_{m=1}^{K}\pi_m
\,\mathcal N\!\left(
\mathbf x_\star;\boldsymbol\mu_m,\Sigma_m
\right),
$$

where each $m$ is a PF solution mode. With one accepted mode this reduces to a single Gaussian. Because the PF modes are inferred from the same uptake data, this is an **empirical-Bayes target model**, not an independent external measurement model. Cross-fitting is required before claiming predictive calibration.

## 4. Data geometry, notation, and non-negotiable preprocessing

### 4.1 Dimensions and canonical vector order

- residues: $r=1,\ldots,R$, with $R=97$ feature residues;
- peptides: $p=1,\ldots,P$, with $P=14$;
- labelling times: $0<t_1<\cdots<t_T$, with $T=15$;
- row-normalised peptide map: $M\in\mathbb R^{P\times R}$;
- observed deuterium fraction: $Y\in\mathbb R^{P\times T}$.

Use time-major vectorisation throughout:

$$
\mathbf y
=
\begin{bmatrix}
Y_{\cdot,1}\\
Y_{\cdot,2}\\
\vdots\\
Y_{\cdot,T}
\end{bmatrix}
\in\mathbb R^{PT}.
$$

The corresponding zero-based flat index is `index = j * P + p`. This ordering must be written into every NPZ manifest.

### 4.2 Intrinsic rates

Let $k_r^{\rm int}$ be the intrinsic exchange rate in min$^{-1}$. Intrinsic exchange depends on sequence context and experimental conditions; sequence-dependent reference-rate work underlies the standard PF construction ([Bai et al., 1993](#ref-bai1993)).

All absolute PF calculations in this project must consume the exact materialised rate source

```text
expfact_historical_pdla_2021
```

with the source file, condition metadata, and SHA-256 recorded. Until that vector is materialised and verified, absolute-number PF results remain rate-source-parametric.

### 4.3 Exchangeable-residue mask

The mapping must exclude residues that do not contribute a measurable backbone amide under the adopted preprocessing, including proline and any N-terminal position treated as unobservable. The following are hard constraints:

- the `-1` PF sentinel is a missing-data marker and must never enter a logarithm or rate equation;
- only 49 of the 97 feature residues are currently resolved by the inverse PF solution;
- one peptide lies outside that resolved overlap;
- unresolved residues must either remain latent under an explicit prior or the affected observation must be excluded from the PF-conditioned target model;
- zero-filling or treating the sentinel as $PF=-1$ is forbidden.

### 4.4 Uptake normalisation and back-exchange provenance

The source materials do not establish unambiguously whether `inputs.observed_uptake` has already been corrected for:

- D$_2$O fraction;
- peptide-specific maximum exchange;
- back-exchange;
- exchangeable-amide count.

The implementation must make these choices explicit in the manifest and must not correct the same effect twice. Peptide-specific maximally deuterated controls are the preferred empirical route for measuring back-exchange and maximum recovery ([Peterle et al., 2022](#ref-peterle2022)). If such controls have already been applied, the peptide-persistent variance term represents residual uncertainty rather than a second correction.

### 4.5 Held-out peptide

The existing project convention that peptide 1 is held out must be carried consistently through:

- PF fitting;
- covariance fitting;
- cross-validation;
- target export;
- downstream reweighting metrics.

A manifest flag must identify whether arrays include the held-out peptide or are already subsetted.

## 5. Mean model: the physical EX2 anchor

### 5.1 Residue-level EX2 uptake

For PF mode $m$, define

$$
z_{rm}=\log PF_{rm},
\qquad
k^{\rm obs}_{rm}=k_r^{\rm int}e^{-z_{rm}}.
$$

Under stationary EX2 exchange, residue uptake is

$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\nu_{rjm}
&=1-\exp\!\left[-k_r^{\rm int}t_j e^{-z_{rm}}\right]
\end{aligned}}
$$

and therefore satisfies

$$
0\leq \nu_{r1m}\leq\cdots\leq\nu_{rTm}\leq1.
$$

This is the primary mean model. It preserves the usual protection-factor meaning: $PF=k^{\rm int}/k^{\rm obs}$. The rate must be evaluated residue by residue before peptide aggregation.

### 5.2 Residue-first peptide mapping

The peptide mean is

$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\mu_{pjm}
&=\sum_{r=1}^{R}M_{pr}\nu_{rjm}
\end{aligned}
}
$$

with $M$ encoding the chosen uptake normalisation. This avoids applying one exponential to an average peptide PF. Residue-resolved and Bayesian interpretations of overlapping peptide HDX data likewise formulate the forward map from residue exchange to peptide observations ([Saltzberg et al., 2017](#ref-saltzberg2017); [Skinner et al., 2019](#ref-skinner2019)).

### 5.3 Optional deterministic calibration

If data provenance demonstrates that a fixed run-level correction remains necessary, use a deterministic calibration in the mean, for example

$$
\mu^{\rm cal}_{pjm}=c_0+c_1\mu_{pjm},
$$

with physically appropriate constraints and strong regularisation. The primary model fixes $c_0=0$ and $c_1=1$ when uptake has already been normalised. A random run-wide rank-one variance is not fitted in the primary model because only one experiment-wide realisation is available and it is confounded with this fixed calibration.

### 5.4 Why free additive increment gains are excluded

The earlier mean wrote exact EX2 increments

$$
\begin{aligned}
\delta_{p,i}
&=\sum_rM_{pr}
\left[\nu_r(t_i)-\nu_r(t_{i-1})\right]
\end{aligned}
$$

and fitted gains $g_i\geq0$ in

$$
u_{p,j}=\sum_{i\leq j}g_i\delta_{p,i}.
$$

Although $g_i=1$ telescopes exactly to EX2 and non-negative gains preserve monotonicity, free gains are not adopted because:

- they act on already integrated uptake rather than on the hazard of the unexchanged population;
- cumulative uptake is not automatically bounded above by one;
- a perturbation to one interval is inherited by every later cumulative point;
- timepoint-specific quench, recovery, or acquisition errors are thereby represented as changes to earlier labelling kinetics;
- they have no direct residue-level PF interpretation.

These experimental effects belong primarily in the covariance model, not in unconstrained cumulative gains.

### 5.5 Optional smooth interval-hazard sensitivity model

A bounded mean-relaxation arm may be retained for model-misspecification testing. For $\Delta t_j=t_j-t_{j-1}$, define

$$
\begin{aligned}
\eta_{rjm}
&=\log\!\left(\Delta t_j k_r^{\rm int}\right)
 -z_{rm}
 +a(\log t_j^\star),
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
q_{rjm}=1-\exp[-\exp(\eta_{rjm})],
$$

$$
\nu_{r0m}=0,
\qquad
\begin{aligned}
\nu_{rjm}
&=\nu_{r,j-1,m}+
 (1-\nu_{r,j-1,m})q_{rjm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Here $a(\log t)$ is a smooth, zero-centred function with only three or four effective degrees of freedom and strong shrinkage toward zero. When $a\equiv0$, the recursion is exactly the ordinary EX2 mean.

The more general coefficient $b_j$ multiplying $\log k^{\rm obs}$ is fixed to one. Allowing $b_j\neq1$ changes the dependence on PF and therefore weakens the meaning of PF itself. It is not part of the primary design.

This sensitivity model is accepted only if it improves held-out predictive density and removes structured residuals. It must be reported as a phenomenological model-discrepancy correction, not as more faithful exchange physics.

For implementation of this arm, let

$$
H_{rjm}=\sum_{\ell\leq j}\exp(\eta_{r\ell m}).
$$

For the general slope notation $\eta_{rjm}=\cdots-b_jz_{rm}$, the sensitivity is

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\partial\nu_{rjm}}{\partial z_{rm}}
&=-\exp(-H_{rjm})
\sum_{\ell\leq j}b_\ell\exp(\eta_{r\ell m}).
\end{aligned}
$$

The accepted sensitivity arm fixes $b_\ell=1$. This derivative must be tested independently from the strict-EX2 derivative.

### 5.6 EX1 and mixed-regime gate

The complete design assumes that centroid uptake is adequately represented by EX2 first moments. EX1 produces a distinct, often bimodal isotopic-envelope signature; mixed EX1/EX2 behaviour may require peak-width or envelope analysis rather than centroid-only fitting ([Weis et al., 2006](#ref-weis2006)).

Before interpreting PFs or the fitted covariance:

1. inspect isotopic envelopes, widths, and evidence for bimodality;
2. flag peptides/timepoints with EX1 or mixed kinetics;
3. either exclude them from this target model or replace the centroid likelihood with an envelope-level or explicit state-mixture model.

Adding more timepoint gains or hazard flexibility is not an acceptable substitute for modelling EX1.

## 6. PF-to-uptake sensitivity and the full joint covariance

### 6.1 EX2 sensitivity

At mode $m$, define

$$
x_{rjm}=k_r^{\rm int}t_j e^{-z_{rm}}.
$$

Then

$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\partial \nu_{rjm}}{\partial z_{rm}}
&=-x_{rjm}e^{-x_{rjm}}
\end{aligned}
}
$$

whose magnitude peaks at $x=1$, the residue's kinetic window.

Let

$$
s_{rjm}=-x_{rjm}e^{-x_{rjm}}
$$

and construct the stacked propagation matrix

$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
A_m[(p,j),r]
&=M_{pr}s_{rjm}
\end{aligned}
}
\qquad
A_m\in\mathbb R^{PT\times R}.
$$

### 6.2 Delta-method propagation

For residue log-PF covariance $C_{z,m}$, first-order propagation gives

$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\Sigma^{\rm PF}_m
&=A_mC_{z,m}A_m^\top.
\end{aligned}
}
$$

The $(j,k)$ peptide block is

$$
\Sigma^{\rm PF}_{m,jk}=J_{jm}C_{z,m}J_{km}^\top,
\qquad
J_{jm}=M\operatorname{diag}(s_{\cdot jm}).
$$

This one term creates:

- covariance between overlapping peptides;
- covariance between structurally coupled but non-overlapping residues after peptide mapping;
- time-dependent marginal variance;
- cross-time covariance caused by the same persistent PF discrepancy;
- a low-rank representation whenever $C_z$ is low rank.

Temporal measurements in HDX form a correlated kinetic trajectory; statistical HDX work has shown the cost of ignoring temporal correlation ([Crook et al., 2022](#ref-crook2022)). Here the cross-time structure follows directly from propagation of one latent PF field through all timepoints.

### 6.3 Nonlinear propagation check

The delta method is a local approximation. For broad or strongly skewed within-mode PF uncertainty, validate it by sampling $z$ from the proposed within-mode distribution, propagating each sample through the nonlinear EX2 map, and comparing the resulting uptake mean and covariance with $(\mu_m,\Sigma_m^{\rm PF})$.

If the discrepancy exceeds a pre-registered tolerance, use nonlinear Monte Carlo moments for that mode. This is particularly important when PF uncertainty spans a residue's kinetic window or when uptake is close to saturation.

## 7. Residue log-PF covariance and structural regularisation

### 7.1 Initial low-dimensional construction

Raw optimizer multistarts are not posterior samples. Their frequencies and covariance depend on initialization and optimizer geometry. Therefore, the initial implementation uses multistarts only to identify modes and a marginal variance profile, not as a calibrated posterior covariance.

For mode $m$, let $D_{z,m}$ be a diagonal, normalised variance-profile matrix. Define

$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
C_{z,m}
&=\tau_z^2D_{z,m}^{1/2}
\left[
(1-\lambda)R_{{\rm base},m}
+\lambda R_{\rm ANM}^{(c)}
\right]
D_{z,m}^{1/2}
\end{aligned}
}
$$

with

$$
\tau_z>0,
\qquad
0\leq\lambda\leq1.
$$

For the initial primary fit,

$$
R_{{\rm base},m}=I.
$$

Thus:

- $D_{z,m}$ supplies the relative residue variance profile;
- $\tau_z$ supplies its absolute amplitude;
- $\lambda$ controls the strength of structural correlation regularisation;
- $R_{\rm ANM}^{(c)}$ supplies only a correlation-shape hypothesis.

Once a bootstrap or posterior calculation provides calibrated within-mode correlation, $R_{{\rm base},m}$ may be replaced by a shrunk $R_{{\rm PF},m}$, with ANM retained as a shrinkage target rather than treated as direct exchange physics.

### 7.2 Why ANM is only a hypothesis

ANM describes correlated Cartesian fluctuations around a reference structure ([Atilgan et al., 2001](#ref-atilgan2001)). Protection factors instead summarize the equilibrium and kinetics of exchange-competent opening and are more directly related, in common structural forward models, to hydrogen bonding and local packing/contact formation ([Best and Vendruscolo, 2006](#ref-best2006)).

Therefore:

- signed ANM displacement correlation is not automatically signed log-PF correlation;
- ANM magnitude must not set the PF variance scale;
- ANM can be a useful low-rank regulariser, but it is not established observation-noise physics;
- identity, diagonal, shuffled-geometry, and sign-control arms are mandatory.

A BV-derived covariance from an independent structural prior ensemble is a more directly PF-linked sensitivity arm than raw ANM. It must not be recomputed from the candidate weights being optimized.

### 7.3 Correlation-sign arms

Let $R_{\rm ANM}$ be a unit-diagonal PSD correlation matrix.

**Signed**

$$
R_{\rm signed}=R_{\rm ANM}.
$$

**Unsigned, PSD-safe**

$$
R_{\rm unsigned}=R_{\rm ANM}\circ R_{\rm ANM}.
$$

The Hadamard square is PSD by the Schur product theorem and retains a unit diagonal. It changes correlation magnitudes by squaring them, so it is an “unsigned magnitude” arm rather than a pure sign flip.

**Domain sign flip**

$$
R_{\rm flip}=SR_{\rm signed}S,
\qquad
S_{rr}\in\{-1,+1\}.
$$

With domains defined before fitting, this preserves all absolute correlations and the full eigenspectrum while reversing cross-domain signs. It is the cleanest pre-registered sign-only comparison.

**Diagonal / identity null**

$$
R_{\rm null}=I.
$$

**Shuffled-geometry null**

Permute the mapping between ANM residues and PF residues while preserving the matrix spectrum. This tests whether any apparent improvement depends on the actual structural registration.

**Legacy absolute-PSD arm**

Elementwise $|R|$ followed by PSD projection may be retained only to reproduce the original draft. Raw $|R|$ is not guaranteed PSD, and eigenvalue clipping can alter magnitudes and eigenvectors; the clipped mass must be reported.

### 7.4 Sign after uptake propagation

For two residues,

$$
\operatorname{Cov}(\nu_r,\nu_s)
\approx
s_rs_s\operatorname{Cov}(z_r,z_s).
$$

Because both EX2 derivatives are negative, $s_rs_s>0$. A signed PF covariance therefore retains its sign before peptide averaging. This makes the sign experiment mathematically meaningful, while the physical mapping from ANM sign to PF sign remains an unverified hypothesis.

## 8. PF non-identifiability and multimodality

Overlapping peptide uptake does not generally determine a unique residue-level PF field. Multiple PF solutions and multimodal residue distributions have been demonstrated explicitly in HDX inverse problems ([Skinner et al., 2019](#ref-skinner2019)).

### 8.1 Mode construction

1. Run `fit_ex2_solution_set` with at least 50 starts, and preferably enough starts to stabilize the discovered clusters.
2. Cluster complete multivariate log-PF solutions within each contiguous overlap region.
3. Recombine compatible regional modes carefully; do not assume independent regional weights without justification.
4. For every mode, store:
   - centre $\bar z_m$;
   - within-mode variance profile;
   - fitted uptake mean $\boldsymbol\mu_m$;
   - objective value and residual diagnostics;
   - uptake-space separation from every other mode.

### 8.2 Mode weights

Multistart frequency is not a valid $\pi_m$. Valid routes include:

- parametric bootstrap of the uptake observations under a fitted experimental-noise model;
- Bayesian posterior sampling;
- an evidence or predictive-likelihood approximation with documented assumptions.

Until weights are calibrated:

- fit and report each accepted mode separately;
- use the best predictive mode for the initial primary implementation;
- use equal weights only as an explicitly labelled sensitivity analysis;
- never interpret optimizer hit counts as posterior probabilities.

### 8.3 Uptake-space relevance

PF modes may be well separated in latent PF space but nearly identical after mapping back to the observed peptide–time surface. Before using a mixture likelihood, quantify pairwise separation, for example with a symmetric Mahalanobis or predictive log-density measure.

If all mode means are indistinguishable relative to $\Sigma_m$, the mixture adds little to uptake-space scoring. Preserve the modes for latent interpretation, but a single uptake target may be adequate.

### 8.4 Mixture target

Once weights are calibrated, the primary reweighting score is

$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal L_{\rm HDX}(w)
&=-\log\sum_{m=1}^{K}\pi_m
\mathcal N\!\left(
\widehat{\mathbf x}(w);
\boldsymbol\mu_m,
\Sigma_m
\right)
\end{aligned}
}
$$

with a stable `logsumexp` implementation.

Moment matching is compatibility-only:

$$
\bar\mu=\sum_m\pi_m\mu_m,
$$

$$
\Sigma_{\rm moment}
=
\sum_m\pi_m
\left[
\Sigma_m+
(\mu_m-\bar\mu)(\mu_m-\bar\mu)^\top
\right].
$$

A moment-matched Gaussian can incorrectly reward the midpoint between two separated plausible basins, so it is not the preferred scientific target.

## 9. Experimental and procedural covariance components

### 9.1 Generative linearisation

Around mode $m$, write the target discrepancy approximately as

$$
\mathbf Y-\boldsymbol\mu_m
\approx
A_m\delta z
+Z_{P,m}b
+Z_{T,m}c
+\varepsilon,
$$

with independent latent effects

$$
\delta z\sim\mathcal N(0,C_{z,m}),
\quad
b\sim\mathcal N(0,\tau_P^2I),
\quad
c\sim\mathcal N(0,\tau_T^2K_T),
\quad
\varepsilon\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma_{\rm exp}^2D_m).
$$

The resulting primary covariance is

$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\Sigma_m
&=A_mC_{z,m}A_m^\top
+\tau_P^2 Z_{P,m}Z_{P,m}^\top
+\tau_T^2 Z_{T,m}K_TZ_{T,m}^\top
+\sigma_{\rm exp}^2D_m
+\epsilon_{\rm num}I
\end{aligned}
}
$$

where $\epsilon_{\rm num}$ is a fixed numerical floor, not a fitted scientific component.

### 9.2 Peptide-persistent term

The primary loading models residual multiplicative peptide recovery/back-exchange uncertainty:

$$
Z_P[(p,j),q]
=
\mathbf 1[p=q]\,\mu_{pjm}.
$$

This creates covariance between all timepoints of the same peptide. A peptide-specific maximum-deuteration correction, when measured, should be applied deterministically first; $\tau_P$ then represents residual uncertainty. HDX reporting recommendations emphasize explicit experimental controls, replicates, and uncertainty reporting ([Masson et al., 2019](#ref-masson2019)).

Sensitivity loading:

$$
Z_P[(p,j),q]=\mathbf1[p=q]
$$

for an additive peptide offset.

The earlier $1/n_{\rm res}(p)$ diagonal arm may be retained as a legacy diagnostic, but it does not represent persistence across a peptide's curve and is not the primary formulation.

### 9.3 Timepoint-common term

A common labelling-time perturbation $\delta\log t_j$ changes each peptide according to its kinetic slope. Use

$$
Z_T[(p,j),k]
=
\mathbf1[j=k]
\frac{\partial\mu_{pjm}}{\partial\log t_j}.
$$

Under strict EX2,

$$
\frac{\partial\mu_{pjm}}{\partial\log t_j}
=
\sum_rM_{pr}x_{rjm}e^{-x_{rjm}}.
$$

Primary choice:

$$
K_T=I_T,
$$

representing separate labelling aliquots whose timing/quench deviations are shared across peptides within a timepoint but independent between timepoints. A fixed smooth kernel over acquisition order or $\log t$ may be tested only if the experimental workflow supports correlated drift.

Sensitivity loading: one for all peptides at a timepoint, representing an additive common calibration shift.

### 9.4 Independent acquisition noise

Let $D_m$ be diagonal. A low-dimensional heteroscedastic family is

$$
d(\mu;\kappa)
=
(1-\kappa)
+
\kappa\left[
\epsilon_u+(1-\epsilon_u)4\mu(1-\mu)
\right],
$$

$$
D_m[(p,j),(p,j)]
=
\frac{d(\mu_{pjm};\kappa)}
{\operatorname{mean}_{p,j}d(\mu_{pjm};\kappa)}.
$$

Then:

- $\kappa=0$: homoscedastic;
- $\kappa=1$: beta-like shape with greater variance near intermediate uptake;
- $\epsilon_u>0$: nonzero floor near zero and saturation;
- $\sigma_{\rm exp}$: global acquisition scale.

This is a parsimonious variance-shape hypothesis, not exact mass-spectrometer physics. Centroid uncertainty also depends on intensity, charge state, envelope overlap, peak picking, and processing. If point-specific standard errors or replicate variances become available, they should replace or anchor this generic shape.

A legacy timepoint-specific diagonal-scale arm may set

$$
D_m[(p,j),(p,j)]
=
s_j\,d(\mu_{pjm};\kappa),
$$

with $\log s_j$ given a strong second-difference penalty and constrained to geometric mean one. This is a high-flexibility diagnostic for residual timepoint variance, not a primary component; otherwise it is strongly confounded with $\sigma_{\rm exp}$, $\kappa$, and the timepoint-common term.

### 9.5 Omitted run-wide random term

A term such as

$$
\tau_{\rm run}^2vv^\top
$$

is omitted from the primary fit. With one experiment-wide realisation it is confounded with fixed target calibration. It may be profiled as a sensitivity arm but must not be freely estimated and then interpreted as repeatability.

## 10. Likelihood, parameterisation, and identifiability

### 10.1 Single-mode likelihood

For residual

$$
r_m=\mathbf y-\boldsymbol\mu_m,
$$

the full Gaussian negative log-likelihood is

$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal J_m
&=\frac12r_m^\top\Sigma_m^{-1}r_m
+
\frac12\log|\Sigma_m|
+
\frac{N}{2}\log(2\pi)
\end{aligned}
}
$$

or its cross-fitted held-out counterpart.

The log-determinant is mandatory. Minimizing only the quadratic permits variance inflation to make every residual cheaper.

### 10.2 Mixture likelihood

For calibrated modes,

$$
\mathcal J
=-\log\sum_m\pi_m
\exp[-\mathcal J_m]
$$

with consistent normalisation constants and `logsumexp` evaluation.

### 10.3 No trace normalisation during fitting

Trace normalisation erases the absolute variance scale and the fitted ratio between covariance components. Therefore:

- fit and compare models with unnormalised $\Sigma_m$;
- preserve $\log|\Sigma_m|$;
- save the fitted absolute scale;
- use trace-normalised precision only for a clearly labelled legacy shape-only loss.

### 10.4 Initial parameter set

The initial full-covariance candidate uses at most

$$
\theta=
\{\sigma_{\rm exp},\kappa,\tau_z,\lambda,\tau_P,\tau_T\}.
$$

Not every component must survive model selection. Fit an ordered hierarchy rather than assuming the most complex model is correct.

Recommended transformations:

- positive scales: log or softplus parameterisation;
- $\kappa,\lambda\in[0,1]$: logistic parameterisation;
- smooth mean sensitivity: zero-centred spline coefficients with a second-difference penalty;
- mode weights: softmax, only when weights are being calibrated by a valid method.

Weakly informative penalties or priors are appropriate because there are no replicate experiments.

### 10.5 Main confounding risks

- $\tau_z$ versus $\kappa$: both can create time-dependent marginal variance;
- $\tau_P$ versus PF discrepancy: both can correlate points within a peptide curve;
- $\tau_T$ versus a flexible time-dependent mean correction;
- run-wide variance versus fixed offset/scale;
- ANM mixing $\lambda$ versus its overall amplitude $\tau_z$;
- mixture between-mode variance versus inflated within-mode covariance.

Required diagnostics:

- profile likelihood or posterior curvature;
- parameter correlation matrix;
- boundary-hit frequency across starts/folds;
- held-out performance after deleting each component;
- stability to numerical jitter and covariance regularisation.

### 10.6 Numerical linear algebra

At $N=210$, a dense Cholesky factorisation is inexpensive and should be the reference implementation. Compute

- the quadratic through triangular solves;
- the log-determinant from the Cholesky diagonal;
- no explicit inverse in the likelihood path.

All scientific covariance components are PSD by construction. Add a small fixed diagonal floor relative to the mean diagonal and report any escalation. Large required jitter is evidence of a model or implementation problem, not a tuning target.

## 11. Cross-fitting and model selection without replicates

### 11.1 Why ordinary in-sample fit is insufficient

The PF modes, their variance profile, and the covariance hyperparameters are inferred from the same uptake surface. An in-sample likelihood will favour flexible target models and cannot demonstrate predictive calibration.

### 11.2 Required outer folds

Use at least two complementary cross-fitting schemes.

**Blocked time folds**

- hold out contiguous timepoint blocks;
- refit PF modes and covariance parameters on the retained times;
- score the held-out peptide–time observations with the corresponding conditional or marginal predictive distribution.

Reuse `_time_folds` from `moprp_covariance_recovery.py` where appropriate, but ensure that PF reconstruction is also refitted inside each fold.

**Peptide/overlap-region folds**

- hold out peptides or contiguous peptide-overlap regions;
- avoid treating heavily overlapping peptides as independent random folds;
- assess whether structural/PF covariance predicts correlations beyond direct peptide overlap.

### 11.3 Primary selection metric

Use mean held-out log predictive density, with uncertainty across folds. Secondary diagnostics include:

- whitened residual structure by peptide and time;
- coverage of predictive intervals where meaningful;
- calibration of Mahalanobis distances in simulation;
- recovery/decoy mass after substituting the frozen target into ensemble reweighting;
- stability of the selected model across PF reference modes.

### 11.4 Model hierarchy

Fit the following nested sequence:

1. diagonal homoscedastic baseline;
2. plus PF-propagated target uncertainty with $R=I$;
3. plus peptide-persistent term;
4. plus timepoint-common term;
5. heteroscedastic $D(\kappa)$;
6. ANM regularisation $\lambda>0$;
7. calibrated PF-mode mixture;
8. smooth hazard mean sensitivity.

A component enters the accepted primary model only when it improves held-out scoring or removes a pre-specified residual failure without destabilising identifiability.

## 12. Integration into jaxENT reweighting

### 12.1 Primary full-joint loss

Add a new loss that consumes a complete target mean and covariance or Cholesky factor:

```text
hdx_uptake_joint_gaussian_loss
```

For a candidate ensemble prediction $\widehat{\mathbf x}(w)$, compute

$$
\frac12
(\widehat{\mathbf x}(w)-\boldsymbol\mu)^\top
\Sigma^{-1}
(\widehat{\mathbf x}(w)-\boldsymbol\mu)
+
\frac12\log|\Sigma|.
$$

When $\Sigma$ is fixed during frame-weight optimisation, the log-determinant is constant with respect to $w$ and may be omitted from each optimization step, but it must remain in covariance fitting and model comparison.

For multiple calibrated modes add

```text
hdx_uptake_joint_mixture_loss
```

using mode-wise Cholesky factors and `logsumexp`.

### 12.2 Freeze target-side uncertainty

Once the target model has been fitted, freeze

- mode means;
- mode weights;
- covariance components;
- covariance scale;
- numerical jitter.

Candidate frame weights must not alter or inflate their own target covariance. Candidate-dependent covariance creates a circular escape route.

### 12.3 Compatibility outputs

**Block-diagonal time-resolved output**

Extract $\Sigma_{jj}\in\mathbb R^{P\times P}$ for each timepoint and form a `(T,P,P)` array. This retains time-varying peptide covariance but discards $j\neq k$ blocks. It is an ablation, not the primary likelihood.

**Collapsed peptide output**

A drop-in `(P,P)` approximation may be constructed from a documented average of marginal blocks. It discards both cross-time covariance and time-dependent precision.

**Trace-normalised precision**

The existing `hdx_uptake_sigma_MSE_loss` expects a trace-normalised peptide precision. Such an export is a shape-only metric. A separate scalar HDX weight, such as $\gamma$, then controls total influence. It must not be called a calibrated Gaussian likelihood.

### 12.4 Delivery hierarchy

1. full $(PT,PT)$ covariance or Cholesky: scientific primary;
2. per-timepoint `(T,P,P)`: block-diagonal ablation;
3. collapsed `(P,P)`: legacy compatibility only.

| Axis | Primary or initial baseline | Sensitivity arms | Explicitly disfavoured |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mean kinetics | strict residue-level EX2 | smooth zero-centred interval offset, PF slope fixed at one | free increment gains; freely varying PF slopes |
| Covariance geometry | full $(PT,PT)$ | block-diagonal `(T,P,P)`; collapsed `(P,P)` | treating timepoints as samples |
| PF base correlation | identity until calibrated | bootstrap/posterior within-mode correlation | raw multistart covariance interpreted as posterior |
| Structural regulariser | fitted ANM mixing with separate amplitude | signed; Hadamard-square; domain flip; BV prior; shuffled geometry | calling ANM displacement covariance “observation noise” |
| ANM absolute arm | not primary | absolute value plus explicit PSD projection | raw elementwise absolute matrix |
| Independent noise | homoscedastic baseline | fitted beta-like shape with floor | claiming $\mu(1-\mu)$ is exact detector physics |
| Peptide effect | multiplicative persistent recovery loading | additive persistent loading; legacy length scaling | independent fresh peptide bias at every time |

## 14. Acceptance criteria and kill rules

### 14.1 Physics and preprocessing gates

- historical intrinsic-rate vector materialised, verified, and hashed;
- residue indexing and units checked;
- exchangeable-residue mask verified;
- no sentinel enters numerical PF calculations;
- uptake normalisation and back-exchange correction documented;
- EX1/mixed-regime screen completed.

Failure of any item blocks absolute PF interpretation.

### 14.2 Mean-model tests

- strict EX2 implementation matches `predict_ex2_uptake` at machine precision;
- interval recursion with $a=0$ matches strict EX2 at all irregular timepoints;
- peptide mapping reproduces the current forward prediction under the same normalisation;
- latent uptake remains in $[0,1]$ and monotone;
- smooth-hazard sensitivity is rejected unless held-out predictive density improves.

### 14.3 Sensitivity and covariance tests

- analytic $\partial\nu/\partial z$ matches central finite differences;
- stacked $A$ has the declared row order;
- every covariance component is symmetric PSD within tolerance;
- the full PF covariance block $J_jC_zJ_k^\top$ matches Monte Carlo propagation for concentrated modes;
- nonlinear propagation is used when the delta approximation fails the pre-registered tolerance;
- signed, unsigned, flip, identity, and shuffled controls preserve their intended invariants;
- absolute-PSD projection reports minimum eigenvalue and clipped spectral mass.

### 14.4 Likelihood tests

- with only diagonal $\sigma_{\rm exp}^2I$, the likelihood reduces to homoscedastic Gaussian MSE plus constants;
- Cholesky and direct reference calculations agree on synthetic small cases;
- mixture log likelihood agrees with a high-precision reference implementation;
- no trace normalisation is applied before likelihood fitting;
- no target covariance parameter depends on candidate frame weights.

### 14.5 Predictive acceptance

The selected model must:

- improve or match blocked held-out log predictive density relative to the diagonal homoscedastic baseline;
- avoid systematic whitened residual patterns by peptide and time;
- show stable parameter estimates and profile curvature across folds;
- require only small numerical jitter;
- remain qualitatively stable across accepted PF reference modes.

### 14.6 Component-specific kill rules

- **ANM:** if $\lambda$ or the ANM-correlated contribution is consistently at zero, or fails to improve held-out prediction versus identity/shuffled controls, reject structural correlation for this system. Do not tune it back in.
- **Peptide term:** if $\tau_P$ is unsupported and maxD correction explains peptide persistence, remove it.
- **Timepoint term:** if $\tau_T$ is unsupported or duplicates a flexible mean correction, remove it.
- **Heteroscedasticity:** if $\kappa$ is unstable or fails held-out comparison, retain homoscedastic acquisition noise.
- **Mixture:** if weights cannot be calibrated or modes are not separated in uptake space, report mode-conditioned results rather than a pseudo-probabilistic mixture.
- **EX2 model:** if envelope diagnostics show substantial EX1, reject this centroid-PF target for the affected observations.

### 14.7 Spectral diagnostics

For every exported covariance, report:

- eigenspectrum;
- fraction of variance in the leading direction;
- effective rank;
- condition number before and after the numerical floor;
- diagonal and correlation heatmaps;
- contribution of each named component;
- comparison with the shipped matrix's reported 90.6% leading-direction variance fraction.

A large leading eigenvalue is not rejected solely because it is large; it must be explained by a named component rather than by a sample-rank artefact.

## 18. Interpretation boundaries

The accepted model can support statements about:

- the relative confidence assigned to different peptide–time directions;
- whether a persistent PF-discrepancy component improves predictive performance;
- whether peptide or timepoint-common effects are supported;
- whether a structural correlation prior helps beyond identity and shuffled controls;
- whether distinct PF inverse modes remain distinct in observable uptake space.

It cannot, without additional data, establish:

- a non-parametric replicate observation covariance;
- that ANM displacement correlation is the true physical covariance of protection factors;
- posterior PF mode probabilities from optimizer hit counts;
- EX2 kinetics for peptides with unexamined or clearly bimodal envelopes;
- independent experimental calibration when the same uptake data were used to infer PFs;
- a universal detector-noise law from the fitted $\mu(1-\mu)$-like variance shape.

Future replicate experiments should be used to anchor acquisition variance, peptide recovery, and run-level effects. The current structured model is a defensible bridge for a non-replicated dataset, not a replacement for replicate measurements.

## 19. Sequencing and out of scope

The following work is intentionally downstream of this design:

- covariance-weighted Jacobian or population-recovery arms run only after the target model passes the physics, numerical, and held-out predictive gates above;
- fast frame-rate averaging is deferred: it changes the candidate forward mean $\widehat{\mathbf x}(w)$, not the target covariance architecture;
- the `fast` forward path remains the default implementation path, with `legacy` retained as a verification arm;
- the wider pivot choice remains open and is not decided by the covariance model alone;
- freely estimated $210\times210$ covariance matrices are out of scope;
- an envelope-level EX1 or mixed-kinetics likelihood is out of scope for this implementation, but its diagnostic gate is mandatory.

Compatibility shrinkage via `shrink_covariance` may be applied to legacy collapsed or block-diagonal exports. Its shrinkage coefficient and spectral effect must be reported. The primary structured joint covariance should obtain positive definiteness from its named PSD components and diagonal acquisition floor rather than from heavy post hoc shrinkage.

## 20. Final mathematical freeze

### 20.1 Primary initial implementation

For each accepted PF mode $m$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mu_{pjm}
&=\sum_rM_{pr}
\left[
1-\exp\!\left(-k_r^{\rm int}t_j e^{-z_{rm}}\right)
\right],
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
A_m[(p,j),r]
&=-M_{pr}x_{rjm}e^{-x_{rjm}},
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
C_{z,m}
&=\tau_z^2D_{z,m}^{1/2}
\left[(1-\lambda)I+
\lambda R_{\rm ANM}^{(c)}\right]
D_{z,m}^{1/2},
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\Sigma_m
&=A_mC_{z,m}A_m^\top
+
\tau_P^2Z_{P,m}Z_{P,m}^\top
+
\tau_T^2Z_{T,m}Z_{T,m}^\top
+
\sigma_{\rm exp}^2D_m
+
\epsilon_{\rm num}I
\end{aligned}
}
$$

with:

- strict EX2 mean;
- $Z_P[(p,j),q]=\mathbf1[p=q]\mu_{pjm}$;
- $Z_T[(p,j),k]=\mathbf1[j=k]\partial\mu_{pjm}/\partial\log t_j$;
- $K_T=I$;
- no free increment gains;
- no freely fitted PF slope $b_j$;
- no random run-wide rank-one term;
- full $210\times210$ likelihood;
- no trace normalisation;
- target covariance frozen during reweighting.

Until mode probabilities are calibrated, the initial primary result is mode-conditioned, with the best held-out-predictive mode used for the main run and all accepted modes reported.

### 20.2 Ultimate target after calibration

After bootstrap or posterior calibration of within-mode correlations and mode weights:

- replace the identity base correlation by a shrunk $R_{{\rm PF},m}$ where supported;
- retain ANM only as a tested shrinkage target;
- score candidate ensembles with the full Gaussian-mixture likelihood;
- retain moment-matched and trace-normalised matrices only as compatibility outputs.

### 20.3 One-sentence design conclusion

$$
\boxed{
\text{strict residue-level EX2 mean}
+
\text{full joint peptide–time structured covariance}
}
$$

is the most physically and statistically defensible centroid-level HDX target for the present MoPrP data, conditional on an EX2 envelope check.
